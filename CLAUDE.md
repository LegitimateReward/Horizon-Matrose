# Random Sailor: editing memory

Standing notes for any Claude session working on Random Sailor videos (Instagram / TikTok).
Read this before editing, captioning or writing descriptions.

## Brand

- Name: **Random Sailor**. The creator stays anonymous: never show or write their real name, face details beyond what they choose, their ship's name, or colleagues.
- Tagline: **Reel Days · River Ways**. Always the pun "Reel", never "Real".
- On-screen signature: `RANDOM SAILOR · REEL DAYS · RIVER WAYS` (Inter SemiBold, letter-spaced, warm cream).
- Description sign-off: `Random Sailor · Reel Days · River Ways`, then hashtags starting `#RandomSailor #ReelDaysRiverWays #ReelDays`.
- Content mix: real POV footage from life on a river cargo ship (Ray-Ban Meta glasses), plus AI cartoon reels of the sailor character (rainbow beanie, mustache, navy jacket). Recurring cartoon cast: the seal, the octopus, the rust dragon, the purple fluffball, the duck, his reflection.
- Remind the user to switch on the AI label (TikTok "AI-generated content", Instagram "AI info") for AI reels.

## Caption style

- Font: InterDisplay Bold, about 60–66 px at 1080×1920, cream `(255,244,220)`, soft dark shadow plus warm glow.
- Usually two short lines, the second fading and rising in about 0.5 s after the first.
- Before placing a caption, check frames: keep it off faces, key action and props. Keep it out of the platform UI zones (top ~12 %, bottom ~20 %, right-side buttons).
- Punchline captions usually land on the final shot. If the last shot has no clean space, show the caption earlier and let the ending play clean.
- Final export: 1080×1920, about −14 LUFS, metadata stripped (Meta glasses files carry device and recording-ID tags).

## Privacy rules for real footage

- Cut, don't blur: remove shots showing the user's own ship name (bow lettering) or colleagues (hi-vis people other than the user's own POV hands).
- Other ships' names and container brands may be shown.
- Drop the original audio when colleagues' voices may be in it.

## Hooks: lessons from @thepostprotocol's "hooks" reel (saved 2026-10-29)

The first 1–3 seconds decide whether people keep watching. Three proven hook types:

1. **Messed-Up Hook** (example: 317M views). Open on something going wrong right away, like a tray of pottery collapsing. People stop scrolling to see the mess.
   - *Random Sailor use:* open on the paint can tipping, rope slipping, the hose spraying back, or the rust dragon melting. Never on calm setup.
2. **Impossible Continuation Hook** (example: 4.5M). Start an ordinary action and let it continue in a way that seems impossible. Viewers stay to see how it can possibly go on.
   - *Random Sailor use:* the needle gun pulls a dragon out of the rust, the paint becomes a mirror, the hose leads into a pipe universe. Show the "impossible" moment within the first second or two.
3. **Cut-Before-Impact Hook** (example: 1.9M). Build to the payoff and cut just before it lands (the smash, the reveal, the punchline word), so the viewer has to keep watching or rewatch.
   - *Random Sailor use:* cut on the sledgehammer's down-swing, or on a caption's first line, before the reveal.

Format techniques the reel itself uses (worth copying):

- **Word-by-word kinetic captions** for talking or voiceover parts: one bold uppercase word at a time, yellow with a dark outline, placed near the speaker.
- **Title card per point**: a thin italic "This is the…" setup line, then a heavy bold keyword ("Messed-Up", "Continuation", "Impact"), plus a small framed proof clip with its view count circled.
- **Keyword-comment CTA** in the caption: "Comment HOOKS and we'll send you 50+ free hooks". It drives comments (7.5k on that reel) and DMs. A Random Sailor version could be "Comment REEL for…".
- **Seamless loop**: the end cuts back into the opening shot, so the replay looks continuous and replays count as watch time.

Default for every new Random Sailor edit: pick one of the three hook types for the first seconds and say which one was used. Keep reels short (about 8–20 s when possible). End with a loop or a reason to follow.
